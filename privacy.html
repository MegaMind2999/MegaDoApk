Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 25, 2026

MegaDo is a personal to-do, notes and reminders app for Android, with a companion app for Windows. It has no accounts of its own, no ads, no analytics and no server. This page explains what data the app handles and where it goes.

Data stored on your device

Your tasks, lists, notes, checklists, photos, drawings, voice recordings and settings are stored only in the app's private storage on your device (on Windows, in your user profile's app data folder). The developer never receives them.

Google Drive sync (optional)

If you turn on sync, MegaDo asks Google for two permissions:

  • drive.appdata: lets MegaDo create, read and delete files in its own hidden folder in your Google Drive. MegaDo cannot see any other files in your Drive.
  • email: your Google account's email address, shown in Settings so you know which account is syncing.

Your synced data goes directly from your device to your own Google Drive. It is never sent to the developer or any third party. You can turn sync off at any time in Settings. To delete the synced copy, open Google Drive on the web, go to Settings → Manage apps, find MegaDo and choose "Delete hidden app data". You can revoke MegaDo's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

MegaDo's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Location

If you create a location reminder, the place you pick is stored with that reminder on your device (and in your Drive if sync is on). Places you save (like Home or Work) are stored the same way. Android's geofencing service watches for you entering or leaving that area. When you come near a place with an arrival reminder, MegaDo checks your GPS position on the phone for a few minutes so the reminder goes off on time, then stops. Your location is not recorded, collected or sent to the developer.

When you search for a place or move the map pin, the place name lookup uses your device's built-in Android geocoder, and map images are loaded from OpenStreetMap's tile servers. Those services receive the map area being viewed or searched, as with any map app.

Phone calendar

Calendar access is asked for only if you turn on "Show phone calendar events" or "Add tasks to phone calendar" in Settings. MegaDo then reads your calendar events to show them in its Calendar screen, and, if you chose a calendar, adds, updates and removes events for your tasks in that calendar only. Calendar data stays on your device and in your own calendar accounts; it is never sent to the developer. Turning the option off removes the events MegaDo added.

Health: water, food, sleep, heart rate and Health Connect

Water, food, sleep and heart-rate readings you log in MegaDo are stored on your device (and in your own Google Drive if sync is on), like your tasks. If you turn on Health Connect in Settings → Health, Android asks you first, and MegaDo can then read your sleep sessions, heart rate and step count, only while its Health screen is open. The only thing it writes to Health Connect is a heart-rate reading you save from the Heart rate tool with “Also add to Health Connect” ticked, and Android asks you before allowing that too. Imported nights and heart rate (kept as one average per hour) are saved in MegaDo like the readings you log yourself; today's steps are only shown, not stored. Health data is never sent to the developer or any third party, and is not used for ads. You can remove MegaDo's access at any time in Health Connect.

Camera, microphone, sensors, notifications and alarms

The camera is used only when you take a photo for a note or while a camera tool is open (QR scanner, magnifier, mirror, heart rate, color picker, reading Morse light flashes). The microphone is used only while you record a voice note, while a sound tool is open (sound meter, tuner, hearing test), or while Live transcription is listening. If you choose captions for phone audio, Android asks you first, and only the sound playing on the phone is used, until you stop. Speech is turned into text on your device; no audio or text is sent anywhere. Motion and other sensors, and GPS for the speedometer and drag timer, are read only while that tool is on screen. Nothing they capture leaves your device. Notification, alarm and full-screen permissions are used only to deliver the reminders, alarms and timers you set.

Link previews in notes

When a note contains a web link, MegaDo loads that page once to show its title and picture in a preview card, as a browser would. The website sees an ordinary request from your device. Pages on your local network are never loaded this way, and previews are kept on your device only. You can turn this off in Settings ("Link previews in notes").

Tools that use the internet

These tools contact a service only while you use them (or, for notifications you turn on, at the times you choose). No account is used and nothing is sent to the developer.

  • Prayer times: the free AlAdhan service receives your approximate location, rounded to about 1 km. An on-device calculation is used when you're offline. The azan reminder, if you turn it on, uses the times already saved on your device.
  • Markets: prices come from Yahoo Finance's public market data; the request contains only the market symbols. If you turn on price notifications, MegaDo fetches only the items you chose, at the times you set.
  • Speed test: downloads and uploads test data to Cloudflare's speed test servers (speed.cloudflare.com).
  • Network: when you tap to show your public IP address, MegaDo asks api.ipify.org, which sees that address and nothing else.
  • Map distance & area: map images come from OpenStreetMap's tile servers, as described under Location.
  • Offline speech to text: when you tap Download, the speech engine comes from MegaDo's GitHub releases and the speech models (OpenAI Whisper, through the whisper.cpp project) from Hugging Face. These are plain file downloads; no audio, text or personal data is sent. After that, transcription works with no internet.

PC remote, remote desktop and Wake PC

These tools talk directly to your own PC over your local network. Pairing details (the PC's name, local address, network card address and remote desktop password) come from the QR code you scan in MegaDo for Windows and are stored only on your phone. On Windows, Phone remote is off until you turn it on; it then listens on your local network and accepts only a phone that sends the pairing code. Remote desktop connects to the remote desktop server built into MegaDo for Windows, which is off until you turn it on. It then listens on your local network and accepts a connection only with its permanent password or a one-time password that changes after each session, and it shares the screen, mouse, keyboard and clipboard only during a connection you or someone you gave the password to opened. If you install the optional "MegaDo Remote Desktop" service, the server also works before anyone signs in, and Windows Firewall is opened for your local network only. Nothing goes through the internet or to the developer.

App updates

Once a day, and when you tap "Check for updates", MegaDo (on Android and Windows) asks GitHub for the latest release of the app. No personal data is sent with this request. Downloaded updates are installed only after you confirm.

Backups

Backups you export are saved where you choose. They contain your data and are not sent anywhere by MegaDo.

Children

MegaDo is a general productivity app and is not directed at children under 13.
